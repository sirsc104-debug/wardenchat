# SirSC's Brain Rocket

A 16:9 quiz game where your rocket only climbs when you answer. Start on the launch pad and fly past the clouds, the Moon, Mars, the outer planets, nebulae and galaxies, all the way to the edge of the universe.

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Open `brain-rocket/index.html` in a browser (or serve the folder with `python3 -m http.server`). The game fills the whole tab at any size with no black bars: its 1600×900 layout always fits and stretches to use the extra width or height, and the world picture behind it keeps its shape and covers the screen. The ⛶ button goes fullscreen. On phones it plays in landscape (holding the phone upright shows a "turn your phone sideways" screen and pauses the game), the game slides up so the answer box stays above the on-screen keyboard, autocorrect is off so it can't change answers, and double-tap zoom and pull-to-refresh are disabled.

## Ways to play

Pick one with the switch on the home screen. Rocket and Submarine each have their own Owen (Easy), Medium, Hard and Ultra Hard modes and their own best scores.

- **🚀 Rocket.** Fly from the launch pad past the Moon, Mars and the outer planets to the edge of the universe. Every question has its own timer; running out of time burns a fuel cell, and the game ends when they're gone.
- **🌊 Submarine.** A timed dive: pick a difficulty, then choose a 30, 60 or 120-second dive in the picker that pops up over the card (each length keeps its own best scores; it works the same for Ultra Hard after you pick its question difficulty). There are no lives and skips are unlimited, so it's all about how deep you get before the clock runs out: past the coral reef, the Titanic, hydrothermal vents and Challenger Deep, then drilling through the crust, the mantle and the core to the other side of the world. The speed bonus counts seconds per question (Owen ×2 within 6 s, ×1.5 within 12 s; Medium 5/10 s; Hard 4/8 s), and the game moves straight on after each answer because the clock never stops. Skipping jumps straight to the next question (and resets your streak).

- **⛏️ Play With Friends** (a drill race). Play against friends without being online together. The host picks a difficulty (Owen, Medium or Hard) and a time (30, 60 or 120 seconds) and makes an **8-character code** (like `JMZQJA4C`). Everyone who types that code gets **exactly the same questions in the same order**, plays on their own, and compares scores afterwards. It plays like Submarine (one clock, unlimited skips), but in its own world: a drill boring down past worms and roots, city pipes, a subway tunnel, dinosaur fossils, crystal caves, the deepest gold mine and the deepest hole ever dug, then through the mantle, the diamond zone and the liquid outer core to the centre of the Earth. The code also carries a check, so a typo is caught instead of quietly starting a different game, and a code only works on the same version of the game (same question bank). Your best on each code is saved, so you can see if you beat yourself.

- **🛗 Elevator** (hidden for now: its tab is hidden in `index.html` and `HIDDEN_TYPES` in `js/game.js`; remove both to bring it back). Ride a glass elevator up the outside of a tower that never ends, in an open sky like the Rocket's. The world around the tower gets stranger the higher you go, with things leaning out of windows, sitting on ledges and floating past, and a big set piece at every milestone: window washers over the city, a jungle floor with swinging monkeys, a floor that is one giant fish tank, a T. rex leaning out of the dinosaur museum, the Cat Kingdom with its king, balls pouring out of the ball pit, a room inside a cloud, a rain room, a sideways room, a pirate ship docked to the tower, floating islands, a circling dragon, a flying library, an astronaut at the edge of space, the Moon parked next door, candy planets, a giant rubber duck and a hall of mirrors full of elevators. The floor counter climbs faster and faster, on to infinity. **Power is your life:** each different wrong answer drains it (Owen −5%, Medium −8%, Hard −12%, Ultra Hard −15%), a timeout costs 20%, and correct answers recharge it a little (+2% for Common up to +10% for Legendary). The tower reacts as power drops: its windows go dark, the lights flicker, the red warning lights blink faster, the car shakes, the floor counter glitches, sparks fly from the cable and a LOW POWER sign flashes. At 0% it's a power outage and the ride is over. On Ultra Hard the elevator sinks between answers.

- **👻 Haunted Flight** (limited time, with a LIMITED TIME badge on its tab that fades from orange into a sliding rainbow and back). While the event runs, the logo wears a witch hat, and spooky background music (a looping D-minor tune made on the fly with Web Audio: music box, organ bass, bells and a wobbly theremin melody) plays whenever the Haunted Flight tab is chosen, on the title screen and in the game. It starts after your first click or key press, follows the 🔊 mute button, and fades down as the ghost gets close so the heartbeat takes over. Outrun a ghost that never stops chasing you. There's no game length, no question timer and no fuel cells: you survive until the ghost reaches your rocket, and your result is how far you flew. The rocket flies right across a Halloween night (the Pumpkin Patch, the Corn Maze, the Graveyard, the Dark Forest, the Witch's Hollow, the Bat Caves, the Haunted Mansion, the Blood Moon, the Spirit Realm and Beyond the Veil) with the ghost coming from behind, and the question card runs along the bottom so the chase gets the whole width.
  - **The chase.** You start 60 m ahead. While a question is up the ghost closes in, starting at 2.5 m/s and speeding up smoothly (+0.9 m/s every minute). A right answer turns its points (rarity × difficulty × streak × deepest cut, with no speed bonus) into metres: on Medium, Common +12 m, Uncommon +24 m, Rare +41 m, Epic +69 m, Legendary +115 m before streaks (the card legend shows the numbers for your difficulty). You can be at most 150 m ahead. You also fly 12 m every second a question is up.
  - **Skips cost distance.** Skips are unlimited, but each one lets the ghost gain 4 seconds' worth of distance. The skip button shows the price before you press it and turns red when a skip would get you caught. Wrong guesses cost nothing extra.
  - **The danger shows.** A "Ghost behind" meter in the HUD and on the card goes green, orange, then red. Inside 40 m the world darkens, the ghost's eyes glow brighter, its hands reach for your fins and a heartbeat gets faster. A right answer under 10 m is a 😱 close escape: the rocket rips free of its grip. Legendary answers blast the ghost into wisps before it re-forms at its new distance.
  - **Fair.** The chase freezes during the countdown and while an answer's result plays. You get one pause per run (switching away from the tab always pauses). When the ghost catches you it wraps around the rocket, its arms curling around the outside of the hull, and the engine flickers out; the results show distance, survival time, correct answers and your closest escape.
  - **Halloween questions.** A quarter of the questions come from a pool of 150 Halloween questions (costumes, candy, monsters, horror movies and villains, famous vampires, witches and ghosts, folklore creatures, scary games and shows, Ghost-type Pokémon, Stephen King books and more, some with "that starts with…" letter rules). The rest are the normal question bank. Halloween questions never come up in the other modes or in Play With Friends codes.
  - **💀 Ultra Hard** (pick Easy, Medium or Hard questions, ×2.5 boosts): the chase never freezes, not even during the countdown or while an answer's result plays (only the pause stops it). And every 8 to 13 seconds of question time the ghost **lunges**: its eyes flash red for 1.5 seconds (with a hiss and an "It's about to lunge! Answer now!" warning on the card), then it dashes 10 m closer. A right answer during the flash cancels the lunge (🛡️ Lunge dodged!). The results show how many lunges you dodged.
  - Owen, Medium, Hard and each Ultra Hard choice keep their own best distance. The event ends on its own: once November 1, 2026 is over (midnight in the player's time zone), the tab disappears and anyone who last played it lands on Rocket. Change `HAUNT_ENDS` in `js/game.js` to move the end date.

- **🎯 Bullseye** (daily). A new set of 15 questions every day, the same for everyone, with a fresh set at midnight in the player's own time zone. Each question names a rarity to **aim for** (three of each, from Common to Legendary, shuffled). Name an answer of exactly that rarity for a bullseye (100 points); every tier off lands one ring further out (60, 30, 10, 5). A timeout or a pass misses the target, and wrong guesses cost nothing. Each question has 30 seconds, and the best day is 1,500 points. You get one go a day: closing the tab or quitting part-way still counts, with the rest as misses. It's played at an archery range (a straw target on an easel, a castle, tents and bunting) to a jaunty folk tune, and arrows stay stuck in the target all game. The results show your target as a 3×5 grid of ring emoji (🎯🟡🔴🔵⚫, ⬜ for a miss) that **📋 Share** copies (or opens the phone's share sheet) with the day's number and score. The Bullseye card on the home screen shows today's number, your day streak, and after playing your score, grid, a Share button and a countdown to the next one. On tall screens it sits under the rules box; otherwise it's a tall card down the left side. Daily questions are plain topic questions (no letter or year rules) from the broad original topics, one per topic, and each must have enough answers at several rarities. Puzzle #1 is October 9, 2026 (`EPOCH` in `js/daily.js`). Everyone gets the same puzzle only while they have the same question bank, so changing topics changes future (and past) puzzles.

**💀 Ultra Hard** is a fourth mode for Rocket, Submarine and Elevator (Haunted Flight has its own Ultra, above). Click its card to pick the question difficulty (Easy, Medium or Hard questions, each with its own best scores). It pays ×2.5 points, but while each question is on screen the rocket falls back down and the submarine floats back up, and the drift gets faster the further you've gone (12 points a second plus 3% of your score). In Rocket, a 🪂 parachute pops out of the nose whenever you start falling inside Earth's atmosphere (below Outer Space), cutting the fall to 30% so it's much harder to drop all the way back to the launch pad. It comes off when you climb again and a new one opens the next time you fall. The submarine has no parachute. Rocket keeps your highest point as your result; Submarine counts where you are when the clock runs out.

## How it plays

- **257 question topics, in three styles** (plus 30 Halloween ones for Haunted Flight).
  - *Topic questions* (most of the game): *What's a breed of dog?*, *Name a type of home security system*, *Name a job that involves operating a vehicle*, *Name a country in Africa*.
  - *Letter questions* (about a quarter): *Name a breed of dog that starts with B and has an N in it.*
  - *Year questions* (rare on Owen, about a third of Hard): *Name an animated movie released between 1990 and 2005*, *Name a US president in office at any time between 1880 and 1890*. They use movies, video games, consoles, inventions, TV shows, books, bands, toys, board games, Olympic host cities and US presidents. A wrong-year answer tells you its year.
- **🐯 DHHS questions.** 22 topics about Daniel Hand High School (Madison, CT), taken from the 2026–27 student handbook: departments, AP and UConn ECE classes, other classes, languages, graduation credits, report-card grades, honours, NHS pillars, periods and lunch waves, the dress code, the phone ban, sick-day symptoms, things you can't throw, snow-day news, parking, fees, rooms, student leadership, school apps, discipline words, and two teacher questions. They come up in every mode (but rarely: 90% less often than other topics), accept common nicknames (APUSH, AP Bio, Calc BC, the caf, Guidance, StuCo, IC…), and several also get letter questions ("Name a room or place at DHHS that starts with C").
- **No spoilers.** A correct answer never shows you the rarer ones you missed. After a skip or a timeout you see one Common answer that would have worked.
- **Fresh games.** The game remembers which topics you've had recently (in this browser), so a new game starts with ones you haven't seen.
- **Niche answers score more.** Every answer has a rarity: Common 10, Uncommon 25, Rare 50, Epic 100, Legendary 200 points. "Labrador" is Common; "Xoloitzcuintli" is Epic.
- **Speed multiplier:** answer in the first quarter of the timer for ×2, in the first half for ×1.5.
- **Streak multiplier:** each correct answer in a row adds ×0.25, up to ×5. Your rocket's flame changes colour at streaks of 3, 5 and 8.
- **Deepest cut:** give one of the rarest possible answers to a question (Rare or better) for an extra ×1.5.
- **Hints on Owen (Easy).** The 💡 Hint button shows the first letter of a Common answer with blanks for the rest (P _ _ _ _); press again for another letter, up to half the word. Each letter costs 20% of that answer's points (one hint ×0.8, two ×0.64…) and ends your streak.
- Wrong guesses are free. Running out of time burns a fuel cell; run out of fuel cells and the game ends. Skips are limited and reset your streak.
- Reusing an answer you already gave this run scores half.
- **No rude words.** Type a swear word or slur as an answer and the run is over: the rocket flips nose-down and speeds up all the way into the Earth (boom), or the submarine turns round and shoots back up out of the sea. You score 0 and no best is saved. Real answers are never treated as rude, so "Blue tit" for a bird, "Ass" for an animal or "Moby Dick" for a book all count as normal.
- Answers are forgiving: small typos ("Chihuahuha"), plurals ("touchdowns"), extra words ("golden retriever dog", "oak tree") and answers inside a phrase ("a big golden retriever") all count.

| Owen (Easy) | 30 s | 5 | 5 | ×1 | Everyday: animals, colours, pizza toppings, toys, the beach… |
| Medium | 20 s | 3 | 3 | ×1.5 | Mixed: jobs, superheroes, cheeses, landmarks, Pokémon… |

The home screen has a small **💬 Warden Chat** button in the bottom-left corner that opens [Warden Chat](https://chat.sirsc104.com/) (another site by the same author) in a new tab.

The home screen keeps the logo and the tab bar in the same place in every mode. Switching tabs slides the highlight to the new tab while the background world slides across in step and the rules box blurs, then sharpens with the new rules.

**Background music.** Every way to play has its own quiet looping tune, made on the fly with Web Audio (no music files): a bright, floaty arpeggio for Rocket, a slow dreamy tune with bubbles for Submarine, a driving dig beat for Play With Friends, a skipping 6/8 folk tune for Bullseye, and lounge "elevator music" for the Elevator. Haunted Flight's spooky tune is louder. The music follows the tab you've chosen, on the title screen and in the game, and starts after your first click or key press. Turn it off (or back on) with **🎵 Music** in the pause menu; that choice is remembered in the browser, and the 🔊 button still mutes everything.

**Startup intro.** The page opens with the SirSC104 producer-tag intro (about 3.7 seconds, with sound made in code). It always starts with a TAP OR CLICK TO START screen (that click is what turns the sound on). A key or click during the animation skips it, and the game doesn't get any input until it's gone. It is the `<script id="sirsc104-intro">` block at the top of `<body>` in `index.html`, copied unchanged from `sirsc-intro.html`: don't edit it.

Keys: **Enter** submits, **Esc** pauses. Best scores per mode are saved in the browser.

## Files

| File | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `js/data.js` | The topic format, the original six topics, and the stops for each journey (`MILESTONES` for Rocket, `DEPTHS` for Submarine, `DRILL_STOPS` for Play With Friends, `ELEV_STOPS` for Elevator, `HAUNT_STOPS` for Haunted Flight) |
| `js/topics-everyday.js`, `js/topics-mixed.js`, `js/topics-expert.js`, `js/topics-more.js` | The other topics with their answers and rarity tiers |
| `js/topics-dhhs.js` | The Daniel Hand High School (DHHS) topics |
| `js/topics-halloween.js` | The Halloween topics that the 150 Haunted Flight Halloween questions are built from |
| `js/topics-extra.js` | Extra answers for 160 topics and 75 more topics (cities in 15 countries, actors, poets, athletes, operas, anime, universities, spacecraft, enzymes, films…), first added with Codex and then re-tiered and cleaned up. City questions come up much less often. |
| `js/topics-years.js` | Topics whose answers carry years, for year questions (`Toy Story @1995`) |
| `js/questions.js` | Picks topic, letter and year questions and checks answers |
| `js/scene.js` | The Rocket world: sky, ground, planets, decorations, rocket, particles |
| `js/drillscene.js` | The Play With Friends world: soil, pipes, subway, fossils, caves, mine, bedrock, mantle and core |
| `js/challenge.js` | Play With Friends codes: packs difficulty, time and a question seed into 8 characters, and seeds the question order |
| `js/elevscene.js` | The Elevator world: the sky, the endless tower, the things around it, the milestone set pieces, the glass elevator and the low-power effects |
| `js/hauntscene.js` | The Haunted Flight world: the Halloween night, its scenery, the sideways rocket, the ghost and the chase effects |
| `js/daily.js` | Bullseye: picks each day's 15 questions and aims from a seed, and keeps today's result, the day streak and the share text |
| `js/bullscene.js` | The Bullseye world: the archery range, the target, arrows in flight and stuck in the target |
| `js/subscene.js` | The Submarine world: ocean zones, sea life, wrecks, the trench, rock, mantle and core |
| `js/badwords.js` | The rude-word check (lists are ROT13-encoded; only runs after an answer is rejected) |
| `js/game.js` | Game flow, scoring, HUD, popups and screens for both ways to play |
| `js/audio.js` | Synthesized sound effects (no audio files) |

To change how rare an answer is, move it to a different tier line in its topic. To add a topic, copy any `topic({...})` block. To rename a mode, edit `MODES` in `js/game.js` and its card in `index.html`.
