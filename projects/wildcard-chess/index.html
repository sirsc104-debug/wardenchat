    Codex

    How it works

    Normal chess, plus a stranger

    Both sides start with the standard army, except that each wildcard (dealt at random from 50) takes the place of one of your regular pieces. It can replace any piece except the king: a pawn, a knight, even your queen. You'll see which one when the cards are dealt.

    You know yours. You don't know theirs.

    Before the game you see your own wildcard and what it does. You also see your opponent's piece and its name, but not its rules. Hover or tap any piece on the board to read about it; theirs only says unknown. Watch what it does and work it out. When the game ends, its rules are revealed and it joins your Codex.

    How to win

    Checkmate still wins. So does destroying the king outright, which some wildcards can do: explosions, charges, arrows. Your own moves can never leave your king in check or get it killed. Stalemate, 50 moves without a capture or pawn move, threefold repetition, and bare kings are draws.

    Playing a move

    Click or drag a piece. Dots mark quiet moves, rings mark captures, and coloured stars mark special abilities. A pulsing ring on the piece itself means it has an ability it uses in place (click it again to use it). If one square offers two different moves, you'll be asked which.

    Promotion

    Pawns that reach the last rank can promote into a queen, rook, bishop, knight, or one of your wildcards.

    The draft

    Pick Draft on the setup screen to build both armies from scratch. You're shown 16 pairs of cards, 8 of them for you to choose from; take one, and the one you leave goes to your opponent. Your army is your 8 picks plus the 8 your opponent passed over, so it might have four bishops and a Cyclops where the pawns usually stand. One pair is always King vs King, so everybody gets exactly one king. You can read the rules of every card offered to you, which means you'll know the wildcards you passed to your opponent, but not the ones they picked. The cards your opponent passes to you stay face down until the draft ends. Afterwards, tap two of your pieces to swap them until the army is set up the way you like. The king always starts on its own square and can't be moved.

    Pass & Play

    Two people can play on one device. Each player looks at their own cards in private, and between turns a screen covers the board until the next player taps Ready, so nobody sees the other's wildcard rules. The board can turn to face whoever is moving.

    Difficulty

    Easy and Medium look one and two moves ahead. Pro calculates four moves deep. Xtreme searches as deep as it can in four seconds and brings one more wildcard than you do. Only Xtreme refuses take-backs.
