    How it works

    Normal chess, plus a stranger

    Both sides start with the full standard army. Each side also gets one or more wildcards, dealt at random from 30, standing on the third rank in front of the pawns.

    You know yours. You don't know theirs.

    Before the game you see your own wildcard and what it does. You also see your opponent's piece and its name, but not its rules. Hover or tap any piece on the board to read about it; theirs only says unknown. Watch what it does and work it out. When the game ends, its rules are revealed and it joins your Codex.

    How to win

    Checkmate still wins. So does destroying the king outright, which some wildcards can do: explosions, charges, arrows. Your own moves can never leave your king in check or get it killed. Stalemate, 50 moves without a capture or pawn move, threefold repetition, and bare kings are draws.

    Playing a move

    Click or drag a piece. Dots mark quiet moves, rings mark captures, and coloured stars mark special abilities. A pulsing ring on the piece itself means it has an ability it uses in place (click it again to use it). If one square offers two different moves, you'll be asked which.

    Promotion

    Pawns that reach the last rank can promote into a queen, rook, bishop, knight, or one of your wildcards.

    Difficulty

    Easy and Medium look one and two moves ahead. Pro calculates four moves deep. Xtreme searches as deep as it can in four seconds and brings one more wildcard than you do. Only Xtreme refuses take-backs.
